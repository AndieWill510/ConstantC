# Session 017 — Second Adversarial Review

## Review Question

Does relationship actually generate constitutional obligation, or is the claim merely true by definition and therefore normatively empty?

## Principal Objections

### Thin relationship was trivial

Authority is necessarily relational in the minimal sense that authority is exercised toward someone or something. That analytic truth cannot by itself generate an obligation. A tyrant and a subject are related in precisely that thin sense.

The review therefore required a thicker account involving such features as mutual vulnerability, dependency, power asymmetry, and capacity to alter another's interests without their full consent.

### The derivation had two primitives

The proposed chain claimed that everything followed from relationship. But repair entered only after fallibility was introduced. Fallibility is not produced by relationship. It is an independent fact about finite decision-makers.

The honest structure appeared to be:

- relationship grounds the claims through due process;
- relationship plus fallibility grounds repair and succession.

### Grounding order and procedural order were conflated

“Gives rise to” was used for several distinct relationships. Obligation may morally entail that standing ought to be granted, but standing may be procedurally prior because no disputed obligation can be heard until someone is permitted to appear.

The review required explicit distinction between metaphysical or moral grounding, epistemic access, procedural sequence, and institutional remedy.

### Standing and contestability needed separation

The review asked what standing grants that contestability does not.

### Succession strained relational grounding

If one party to a relationship disappears, does the relationship—and therefore the obligation—also end?

Two theories were identified:

1. The obligation, once generated, becomes free-standing.
2. A successor enters a new relationship and thereby assumes some obligation.

### The scope problem moved rather than disappeared

If relationship triggers the entire framework, nearly every institutional action may place someone in diffuse relational proximity to power. A threshold was still needed.

### The ontology claim lacked a test

Terms such as *primitive*, *ontology*, and *emerges* risked canonizing a beautiful formulation before it had earned constitutional weight. The review requested falsifiable tests and counterexamples.

## Narrow Direction from Review

> What about a relationship, specifically, generates obligation?

The response proposed consequence-bearing relationships.
