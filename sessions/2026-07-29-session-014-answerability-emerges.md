# Session 014 — Answerability Emerges

## Working Hypothesis

Repair appeared to be one expression of a larger constitutional commitment.

> Legitimate authority remains answerable.

Answerability seemed capable of organizing several commitments already present in the work:

- standing;
- contestability;
- due process;
- repair;
- succession.

Repair addressed what happens after established harm. Standing addressed who may appear. Contestability addressed whether authority may be challenged. Due process addressed how a challenge is heard. Succession addressed whether obligations survive institutional change.

## Progress

This move explained why repair mattered without making repair the sole source of legitimacy.

Repair preserved legitimacy because legitimate authority remained answerable after error.

The move also replaced an apparently ad hoc collection of duties with a proposed genus: each commitment expressed a different way in which authority remained reachable.

## Provisional Definition

Answerability was initially described through its manifestations rather than independently defined.

That produced a circular risk:

- answerability was what standing, contestability, due process, repair, and succession expressed;
- the five commitments mattered because they expressed answerability.

The formulation could organize the material, but it did not yet ground it.

## Remaining Question

What is answerability apart from the commitments through which it appears?

The search for an independent ground led toward relationship.
