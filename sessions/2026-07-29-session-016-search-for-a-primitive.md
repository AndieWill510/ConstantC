# Session 016 — Search for a Primitive

## Working Hypothesis

The response to the first review proposed that answerability was not primitive.

The deeper candidate was **relationship**.

The proposed derivation was:

relationship → obligation → standing → contestability → due process → repair → succession.

The intuition was that authority becomes answerable because authority exists in relationship. Relationship creates obligation; obligation requires standing; standing enables contestability; contestability requires due process; fallible process requires repair; and unresolved obligations require succession.

## Why the Proposal Was Attractive

It offered an independent ground beneath answerability.

It also resonated with a longstanding ConstantC and TinyDoor formulation:

> We belong in one another, not to one another.

Relationship, rather than ownership, appeared capable of grounding authority without reducing people to objects of administration.

The proposal also clarified that explanation alone was insufficient. Relationship implied reciprocity, and reciprocity suggested revisability.

## Provisional Definition

> Answerability is the constitutional expression of relationship under conditions of authority.

And an even deeper opening sentence was considered:

> Authority exists only within relationship.

## Unresolved Risk

The word *relationship* was doing too much work.

In a thin descriptive sense, every authority has an addressee. But a tyrant is also related to a subject. Mere relational grammar cannot generate obligation.

For relationship to carry normative force, it needed to be thickened.

That challenge became the center of the next review.
