# Status: Legitimate Authority and Answerability

**Constitutional status:** Working inquiry  
**Canonical status:** Not canon  
**Current synthesis:** [`001-legitimate-authority-and-answerability.md`](001-legitimate-authority-and-answerability.md)  
**Deliberative record:** [`thinking/`](thinking/)

## Present Position

The current inquiry proposes that legitimate authority remains answerable to those whose lives it can materially affect.

That position has survived several rounds of revision, but its grounding remains incomplete. The work has moved through repair, answerability, relationship, and consequence-bearing power without yet establishing a final constitutional primitive.

The present essay therefore records the strongest synthesis available now. It does not claim finality.

## What Has Been Earned

The inquiry currently distinguishes:

- descriptive relationship from consequence-bearing relationship;
- moral grounding from procedural access;
- standing from contestability;
- explanation from responsiveness;
- repair from the creation of legitimacy;
- continuity of obligation from continuity of institutional identity.

It also recognizes that consequential power and institutional fallibility are descriptive premises. A separate normative bridge is required to derive an obligation of answerability.

## Questions Still Open

1. Does a consequence-bearing relationship arise from an authority's capacity to affect, from an exercise of that capacity, or from both under different thresholds?
2. What normative principle converts consequential and fallible power into an obligation to account, revise, and repair?
3. How should asymmetry and vulnerability be defined and weighted?
4. What threshold makes an effect constitutionally significant rather than merely diffuse or incidental?
5. Are standing, contestability, due process, repair, and succession exhaustive expressions of answerability?
6. Which forms of continuity transfer unresolved obligations to successor institutions?
7. Can an unresolved obligation divide among multiple successors?
8. How does this framework reconcile with prior ConstantC work that may identify standing as a first covenant?

## Rule of Restraint

No claim in this section should be treated as canon merely because it is elegant, coherent, or repeatedly stated.

The inquiry remains open until its premises, bridges, scope, counterexamples, and relationship to existing canon have been tested.
