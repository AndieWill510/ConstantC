# Thinking Record 002: Repair Before Decisions

## Working Hypothesis

The first substantial formulation treated repair as more than a remedy after failure.

It proposed that legitimate authority must be repairable before it acts. An institution should not wait for harm to discover whether it has any constitutional means to acknowledge, revisit, correct, or compensate.

The core intuition was:

> Repair must exist as a prior capacity, not merely as a later gesture.

## What This Formulation Achieved

It rejected perfection as the basis of legitimacy.

It also rejected apology as a sufficient account of repair. Repair could require changed records, restored access, restitution, institutional reform, or truthful accounting where restoration was impossible.

Most importantly, the formulation treated repair as structural. A system that makes correction impossible has designed its own fallibility out of constitutional view.

## Constitutional Challenge

The difficulty was categorical.

Was repair:

- what creates legitimacy;
- what legitimate authority is obligated to do;
- or what preserves legitimacy after error?

The first language moved among these claims without distinguishing them.

If repair creates legitimacy, then legitimacy appears to arise only after failure. If repair is merely a duty imposed on already legitimate authority, its source still requires explanation. If repair preserves legitimacy, then some prior feature must explain why legitimacy existed and why repair became owed.

## Revision Forced by Review

The inquiry adopted a narrower claim:

> Repair does not create legitimacy. Repair preserves the possibility of legitimacy after fallibility becomes actual.

That revision solved one problem but opened another.

If repair is downstream, what broader constitutional principle generates the duty to repair?

The search moved from repair to answerability.
