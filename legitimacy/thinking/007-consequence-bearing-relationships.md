# Thinking Record 007: Consequence-Bearing Relationships

## Revision

Relationship simpliciter was abandoned as too thin.

The inquiry proposed a thicker formulation:

> Authority creates a consequence-bearing relationship whenever it has the power to alter another person's conditions, standing, rights, opportunities, or possibilities.

The obligation did not arise from connection alone. It arose from consequential power operating under conditions of asymmetry and vulnerability.

## Two-Premise Structure

The response also accepted that the argument did not rest on one primitive.

It identified two descriptive premises:

1. Authority acts through consequence-bearing relationships.
2. Finite decision-makers are fallible.

The provisional conclusion was:

> Because authority can materially affect others and can be wrong, legitimate authority must remain open to challenge, revision, and repair.

This was more honest than deriving fallibility from relationship.

## Differentiated Commitments

The earlier chain was replaced with several distinct constitutional relationships.

- **Obligation** is morally grounded in consequential power.
- **Standing** is the procedural recognition that a person may bring a claimed obligation into view.
- **Contestability** ensures that the authority, decision, or outcome may genuinely be challenged.
- **Due process** governs how the challenge is considered.
- **Repair** addresses what becomes owed when harm or error is established.
- **Succession** determines where an unresolved obligation goes when its original institutional bearer changes or disappears.

This distinction clarified standing and contestability:

> Standing answers: who may appear and be heard?
>
> Contestability answers: what may be reopened, questioned, or changed because they appeared?

## Succession Revised

Succession was reframed as constitutional inheritance rather than metaphysical survival of the same relationship.

A successor might inherit unresolved obligation where it inherited some relevant continuity, including authority, role, benefit, assets, records, jurisdiction, or practical capacity connected to the harm.

## Provisional Formulation

> Legitimate authority is power exercised within a consequence-bearing relationship, under conditions of fallibility, while remaining structurally open to account, challenge, revision, and repair.

## Tests Proposed

The response proposed tests concerning:

- which relationships generate obligation;
- whether consequential authority can be legitimate without revisability;
- whether explanation can coexist with constitutional unresponsiveness;
- when diffuse impact becomes too remote for standing;
- what continuity transfers unresolved obligation;
- what counterexample would defeat the proposed ground.

The next review accepted much of this revision but found several hidden gaps.
