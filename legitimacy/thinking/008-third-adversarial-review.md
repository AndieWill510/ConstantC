# Thinking Record 008: Third Adversarial Review

## Review Question

Did the consequence-bearing relationship revision actually close the argument, or did it relocate unresolved problems?

## What the Review Accepted

The review accepted several genuine advances:

- the tyrant counterexample had been defeated;
- standing and contestability had been successfully distinguished;
- succession had been reframed as inheritance through continuity rather than persistence of the same relationship;
- the framework now acknowledged two descriptive premises rather than pretending fallibility followed from relationship.

## Principal Objections

### Capacity and exercise were conflated

The formulation alternated between authority that *has the power* to alter another's conditions and power that has actually been *exercised*.

Those produce different scopes.

If capacity alone is sufficient, a central bank stands continuously in a consequence-bearing relationship with everyone in its currency zone. If exercise is required, obligations may arise only after a particular decision lands.

The distinction affects diffuse impact, standing thresholds, and the reach of institutional duties.

### The normative bridge remained missing

Consequential power and fallibility are descriptive facts.

No conjunction of descriptive premises entails the normative conclusion that authority owes account, revision, or repair.

A further premise was required, such as:

> Those who may be materially affected by power that may err are entitled to meaningful means of redress.

The review identified this as likely the actual normative core of the framework.

### Asymmetry and vulnerability disappeared in compression

The fuller definition relied on consequential power under conditions of asymmetry and vulnerability. The compact version said only that obligation was grounded by consequential power.

The review asked whether asymmetry and vulnerability were independently necessary, typically present, or already contained in the meaning of consequential power.

### Exhaustiveness became more urgent

The provisional definition of legitimate authority named account, challenge, revision, and repair. These corresponded closely to the commitments the inquiry was attempting to justify.

That circularity was not necessarily vicious, but it made the list's completeness load-bearing.

The review asked whether transparency, proactive disclosure, consent, proportionality, or another form of structural openness might exist outside the current list.

### Succession needed a weighting rule

Authority, role, benefit, assets, records, jurisdiction, and practical capacity were presented as though any one might suffice.

But continuity of records alone should not necessarily transfer the full obligation to repair. Benefit or inherited authority may carry greater weight.

The review also raised fragmented succession: one institution may inherit assets, another jurisdiction, and another practical capacity.

The philosophical questions became:

- Are forms of continuity equally weighted?
- Are some sufficient alone and others only in combination?
- Can unresolved obligation divide among several successors?

### Prior canon might be reconcilable

Standing could remain “first” in procedural access even if obligation were prior in moral grounding.

This hypothesis required checking against the actual standing article rather than relying on recollection.

## Tests Added

The review added two explicit tests:

- **Bridging-premise test:** What normative principle converts consequential and fallible power into an obligation of answerability?
- **Exhaustiveness test:** Is there a form of structural openness outside the current set, and would finding one defeat or extend the framework?

## Narrow Direction from Review

The review recommended resolving capacity versus exercise first, because that answer silently sets the scope of standing, diffuse impact, and succession.

The current essay records the issue rather than pretending it has been resolved.
