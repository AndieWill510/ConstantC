# Thinking Record 009: Open Constitutional Questions

The present essay is intentionally incomplete. This record gathers the questions that must remain visible while the work continues.

## 1. Capacity or Exercise

Does a consequence-bearing relationship arise because an institution possesses consequential capacity, because it exercises that capacity, or through a tiered model?

A tiered account may distinguish:

- baseline duties created by capacity, such as restraint, disclosure, preparedness, and accessible channels;
- specific duties created by exercise, such as notice, reasons, reconsideration, and repair.

This possibility has not yet been tested.

## 2. The Normative Bridge

What principle converts these descriptive premises—authority can affect and authority can err—into an obligation of answerability?

The current candidate is:

> Those exposed to consequential power that may err are entitled to meaningful means of account, challenge, revision, and redress.

Why is that entitlement justified? Is it grounded in reciprocity, dignity, non-domination, equality, consent, vulnerability, justice, or another principle?

## 3. Consequence Threshold

What counts as a material or constitutionally significant effect?

How should the framework treat:

- diffuse effects;
- probabilistic effects;
- cumulative effects;
- indirect effects;
- future effects;
- effects on communities rather than identifiable individuals?

## 4. Asymmetry and Vulnerability

Are asymmetry and vulnerability necessary conditions, evidentiary indicators, or common consequences of institutional power?

Can two peers stand in reciprocal consequence-bearing relationships? If so, are their duties symmetrical?

## 5. Standing and Prior Canon

Does existing ConstantC canon identify standing as the First Covenant?

If so, can the present framework reconcile that status by distinguishing:

- obligation as prior in moral grounding;
- standing as first in procedural access?

The actual canon must be fetched and read before this question is answered.

## 6. Responsiveness

What minimum possibility of revision must exist for answerability?

Some decisions cannot be reversed. Some harms cannot be repaired fully. Some authorities lack unilateral power to change an outcome.

Can truthful account plus constrained remedial action satisfy answerability where full revision is impossible?

## 7. Exhaustiveness

Are standing, contestability, due process, repair, and succession a complete set?

Where do transparency, proactive disclosure, consent, proportionality, recusal, representation, and explanation belong?

The list should remain open until a principled account of inclusion and exclusion is developed.

## 8. Succession and Continuity

Which forms of continuity transfer full obligation, partial obligation, or only a narrower custodial duty?

Potential forms include:

- inherited authority;
- inherited role;
- inherited benefit;
- inherited assets;
- inherited jurisdiction;
- inherited records;
- inherited practical capacity.

How should obligations be handled when continuity fragments across several successors?

## 9. Divisibility of Obligation

Can unresolved constitutional obligation be divided?

Possibilities include:

- proportional division;
- joint responsibility;
- differentiated duties according to capacity and benefit;
- indivisible obligation held concurrently by several institutions.

## 10. Counterexamples

What would defeat the framework?

The inquiry should search for:

- legitimate authority with no identifiable consequence-bearing relationship;
- consequential authority that owes no answerability;
- a relationship involving asymmetry and vulnerability that generates no constitutional obligation;
- answerability that requires none of the current commitments;
- a form of structural openness omitted by the framework.

## Current Restraint

These questions are not a queue to be answered cosmetically.

They are constitutional pressure points. Any future revision should identify which question it answers, what premise it changes, and what new consequences follow.
